## Findings

### MATERIAL 1: the authority cross-check compares against contaminated lab rows, and the "token program disagreement" is mis-described

27 of the 33 `token_authorities` rows in `data/memelab.sqlite` (source `merged`, 7 of the 8 mints: `55Vr2VpS`, `6GmAFSYs`, `7KEPApdb`, `9XKzy4Ka`, `DoVAVzVi`, `HcRLc9VD`, `Hg5Ja55T`) carry in `extensions_json` the `tokenMetadata` and `metadataPointer` of another mint, `98kfF7rmsg1QDUEoCqNE7g7M1FdrTt92TEp2CLzypump` ("Paid", PAID), and the Token-2022 program id. A tokenMetadata extension names its own mint, so these rows mix two identities (P01). Supply and decimals still differ per mint, so only part of each row is copied. For `6GmAFSYs` the lab's own `tokens` table says SPL Token (`Tokenkeg...`), the same as the token row of every one of its 51 decisions in the p05 store; only the contaminated authority row says Token-2022.

The phase reports "authority 33 of 33 agree" (card, SOURCE_LEDGER, EVIDENCE, HANDOFF) and "the token program of one mint disagrees ... which is right is UNKNOWN without a chain read" (card), and PI-D-055 item 4 tells later phases to carry "the lab DB and the export disagree on the token program of one mint". The evidence points the other way: the lab DB authority rows are contaminated, the export and the lab's own token table agree, and only 6 rows (the mint `98kfF7rm` itself) are a clean reference. The 33-of-33 agreement is not an independent confirmation (all authorities are revoked on every side, and 27 rows may be another mint's read). No model is affected (`oc_token2022` and authority features enter no model, 0 of 240 active), but the record would mislead later phases that use the lab DB as a reference. Repair: an identity check in the cross-check (metadata mint equals row mint), report 6 clean rows, describe the token-program case as lab DB contamination with the lab's own token table agreeing with the export, and correct PI-D-055, the card, the ledger, EVIDENCE and HANDOFF.

### MATERIAL 2 (contract, new counterexample CX-S09): the depth-only size cap enforces neither time, pool nor recency

`onchain.size_cap_usd(quotes, max_loss)` and `decision_support(forecast, flags, quotes, max_loss)` take no decision time and no pool. They take the largest OK size over whatever rows they are given, so a collector read captured after the decision (CX-09), a quote from another pool of the mint (P02) and a 19 h old quote all count, and an older good quote outranks a newer bad one at the same size. The docstring says the rows must be "visible at the time of use", but nothing enforces it, and the natural call passes `depth_adapter.observations(con, chain, mint)` straight in. The card states CX-09 is refused by `visible_at`; that holds for features and `exit_context`, not for the size cap, which phase step 5 names as the execution guard. No test or mutation in the phase covers this. See the counterexample below. Not material to any number today (0 collector rows, every cap EXECUTION_DATA_MISSING), but a later phase wiring decision support would inherit a cap that can leak and overstate depth. Repair: give `size_cap_usd` and `decision_support` the decision time and pool, call `assert_visible`, keep only the newest visible OK quote per size on that pool (with a maximum age), and add a test with this fixture.
